NVDA vs the AI Complex: 25 Days Is a Different Trade

By AI Market Cap 2026-10-23 #NVDA pre-earnings setup

NVIDIA reports on a listed November 17 date, 25 days out. Almost everything else in the mega-cap AI complex that people are trading this month reports within five or six days: Microsoft and Alphabet on October 28, Amazon on October 29, Arista and AMD on November 3. NVIDIA is not part of that cluster, and treating it as though it were is the most common mistake in pre-earnings AI positioning right now.

The Scanner Numbers

The live scanner has NVIDIA at a score of 91, a price of $211.53, and a market cap of $5,107.81B, the largest in the universe. The analyst book is 62 covering with 10 strong buy, 49 buy, 2 hold, 1 sell. Short interest is 1.3% of float and the sentiment reading is Positive.

The implied-volatility reading is 43.6. That number is the reason the 25-day window matters. Compare it to ServiceNow at 96.7, Vertiv at 110.1 and Datadog at 98.0, all of which report inside twelve days. NVIDIA is priced for a materially calmer reaction than the short-window names, which is exactly what you would expect from a company whose outcome the market has largely stopped being surprised by.

How NVIDIA Behaved Through the Selloff

On October 22 the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 6.72% and the iShares Semiconductor ETF fell 6.79%. NVIDIA fell 2%. It went from $221.97 to $212.21, a decline of 4.39% on the day, and it has since recovered to roughly $213 intraday on October 23.

Over roughly the past month NVIDIA is down 5.34%. The semiconductor complex over the same stretch is down considerably more: SOXX at 16.1% and SMH at 12.75%. Lam Research, the company whose commentary triggered the whole episode, is down 27.9% over the month. AMD, which had not reported at the time, fell 8.10% on October 22 with its print eleven days away.

So NVIDIA absorbed a sector event it had nothing to do with, lost about 4% on the day, and gave back roughly 5% over the month. That is the signature of the largest, most liquid name in the group absorbing a repricing meant for somebody else.

What the Targets Say About Time Decay

The scanner's target ladder for NVIDIA is unusually flat. The 3-day target is $214.52, about 1.4% above the current price. The 7-day target is $220.52, about 4.2% above. The 14-day target is $226.50, about 7.1% above.

Look at how that compares to Datadog at 98.0 implied volatility, where the ladder runs 4.6%, 13.7% and 22.9% across the same three horizons. Both are high-quality setups. One expects a 1.4% move in three days and the other expects 4.6%, and that difference is almost entirely the implied-volatility reading rather than any judgment about company quality.

The practical consequence is that a long-dated NVIDIA structure and a long-dated Datadog structure are not the same trade wearing different ticker symbols. NVIDIA's premium will be materially cheaper and its expected payoff materially smaller. That is the correct trade if you want calm AI beta; it is the wrong trade if you need a move.

Why 25 Days Is Structurally Different

Two things happen in a long pre-earnings window that do not happen in a short one. The first is decay: if the event does not arrive within the structure's life, the position bleeds premium toward zero regardless of direction. The second is re-marking: in 25 days, the entire macro backdrop can change twice over, and on October 22 alone the AI complex lost roughly $200B in a session on nothing company-specific.

Neither problem exists in the same form for a name 5 days out. A five-day position has little time to decay and little time for the world to change underneath it. That is not an argument that the short window is better. It is an argument that the two windows are different instruments and should be sized and structured differently.

Why the Market Prices NVIDIA Differently

The reason implied volatility sits at 43.6 for NVIDIA and 96.7 for ServiceNow is not that traders expect worse news from NVIDIA. It is that the outcome distribution is narrower, and the reason for that is position size. NVIDIA's market cap of $5,107.81B is the largest in the universe. A surprise moves a smaller percentage of a bigger base than the same surprise moves in a $162.98B company.

That is also why the drawdown was smaller. On October 22 NVIDIA fell 4.39% while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 6.72% and Lam Research fell 11.76%. A name with 62 analysts covering it, 10 strong buy ratings and $5.1 trillion of market cap does not get repriced as violently as a smaller one on the same news, because the buyer base is deeper and the marginal seller is less able to move the price.

Sentiment and the Sell Side Count

The analyst book is 62 strong buy, buy, hold and sell ratings combined: 10 strong buy, 49 buy, 2 hold, 1 sell. That is 59 of 62 non-negative, or about 95%. The single sell rating is not a meaningful dissent block, and neither is the two holds.

Earnings sentiment reads Positive, meaning the company has been beating against consensus recently. For a company of NVIDIA's size that is close to a precondition rather than an edge, and it is a fair reason the score is 91 rather than 98. Datadog scores 98 with a Positive reading too, but on a 48-analyst book covering a company with roughly 1/55th the market cap. The score compresses differently by size.

The Cross-Check on the Long Window

If a 25-day window feels long, the cross-check is what else is printing inside it. In the four weeks between now and November 17, the universe contains Amazon on October 29, Arista and AMD on November 3, Constellation Energy on November 9, and Alphabet and Microsoft on October 28. Each of those prints will move the complex that NVIDIA trades inside.

That is the real risk in a long pre-earnings window on a mega-cap. You are not just exposed to NVIDIA's November 17 print. You are exposed to five other events that will each set the tone for the complex before you get there, and each of them is a repricing opportunity or a repricing risk that has nothing to do with NVIDIA's quarter.

Key Takeaways

  • NVIDIA sits at a score of 91 with 25 days to a listed November 17 print, at $211.53 and a $5,107.81B market cap.
  • Implied volatility reads 43.6, far below ServiceNow at 96.7, Vertiv at 110.1 and Datadog at 98.0.
  • It fell 4.39% on October 22 against a 6.79% decline in SOXX, and is down only 5.34% over roughly a month.
  • Its target ladder is flat at 1.4%, 4.2% and 7.1% across the 3-day, 7-day and 14-day horizons.
  • Sixty-two analysts cover it with 10 strong buy, 49 buy, 2 hold and 1 sell, and short interest is just 1.3%.

What to Do Next

If your view is that AI compute demand stays intact over the next month, NVIDIA is the cheapest liquid way to hold it and the structure most exposed to decay and to a macro re-rating. If you want to be right within a week, the five-day names are the right instruments and NVIDIA is not a substitute for them.

Watch whether the semiconductor complex stabilises out of the October 22 lows. SOXX at 475.69 and SMH at 523.35 are the reference points, and both have recovered slightly intraday on October 23. A complex that keeps bleeding will not wait for NVIDIA's November 17 date to reprice it.
