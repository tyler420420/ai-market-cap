NOW Stock: The One AI-Adjacent Name That Kept Rising

By AI Market Cap 2026-10-23 #NOW stock pre-earnings

On October 22 the iShares Semiconductor ETF fell 6.79%, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost 6.72%, and roughly $200B of market value came out of thirty companies. ServiceNow closed that day at $157.00, up from $149.54 on October 20. It is one of the very few names in technology that went the other way during the worst sector session of the year, and it reports in 5 days.

The Outlier Position Is Real and Measurable

Over roughly the past month ServiceNow is up 15.36%. Over the same stretch, SOXX is down 16.1%, SMH is down 12.75%, and the S&P 500 is down 1.43%. A 15% gain against a 16% decline in the semiconductor complex is not noise and it is not a one-day artifact. The path was steady: $149.54 on October 20, $153.12 on October 21, $157.00 on October 22, and roughly $156 intraday on October 23.

ServiceNow rose through the same Lam Research supply-chain shock that repriced the entire AI infrastructure trade. That is the observation worth carrying into the print. The market decided this month that the AI theme had become a financing and supply story, and ServiceNow was not part of that repricing.

Where It Sits in the Scanner

The live scanner has ServiceNow at a score of 93, a listed October 28 earnings date with 5 days left, a price of $157.64 and a market cap of $162.98B. Forty-nine analysts cover it: 9 strong buy, 35 buy, 3 hold, 2 sell. Short interest is 2.9% of float, which is a normal level and not a squeeze setup.

Score 93 puts ServiceNow in the top ten of a 100-name universe. What separates it from the rest of that tier is the implied-volatility reading of 96.7, which is more than double where Microsoft sits at 25.6 and roughly triple Amazon at 36.8, while still sitting below the very highest readings on the board.

The Problem With Buying a Winner Here

Let us be direct about the risk, because the last two paragraphs were bullish and the setup is genuinely two-sided.

ServiceNow has already run 15.36% in a month. Going into a print, the pre-earnings buyer is now paying for that momentum. A 15% run compresses the asymmetry: you are buying after the easy repricing has happened, at a higher price, with the same event still ahead. The scanner's 3-day target is $161.60, about 2.5% above the current price, the 7-day target is $169.52, about 7.5% above, and the 14-day target is $177.44, about 12.6% above.

At 96.7 implied volatility, those targets are wide, and wide means the premium is expensive. The scanner's 3-day expectation of 2.5% is the honest number for a short-dated structure. Anything that needs the 12.6% fourteen-day move to pay for itself is a bet on the tail, not on the setup.

Why the Resilience Might Be Structural

There is a defensible reason ServiceNow was not sold with the AI complex, and it is worth stating rather than just celebrating the chart. The names that fell hardest in October were the ones whose demand story required continuous capital to be raised and equipment to be delivered. ServiceNow sells software seats and workflow subscriptions. Its revenue is not gated by GPU supply or by clean-room availability, and Jensen Huang's characterization of neocloud demand as "off the charts" on October 22 says nothing bad about enterprise software demand.

That is a coherent reason for relative strength. It also means the print will be judged on its own numbers rather than on the week's macro mood, which cuts both ways: no sector sympathy if software multiples compress, but no sympathy drag either.

How ServiceNow Compares With the Rest of the Software Cohort

The divergence inside software is as striking as the divergence between software and semiconductors. Over roughly the past month ServiceNow is up 15.36%. Mastercard is up 9.91%. Microsoft is up 1.02%. Alphabet is down 5.97%. Palantir, at a score of 72 with a listed November 2 date, is up 3.06%.

ServiceNow is the strongest name in that group by a wide margin, and it achieved that while the semiconductor complex lost more than 16%. A stock does not usually run that hard into a print without the market already having a reason, and the reason here appears to be business model rather than sympathy. These are subscription and seat businesses. Their revenue is not gated by clean-room availability, delivery lead times or GPU allocation, which is precisely the constraint Lam Research described.

Two consequences follow. The first is that there is no sector read-through to lean on, in either direction. If software multiples compress on a rate move, ServiceNow goes with them. The second is that the print will be judged on its own subscription metrics rather than on a week's macro mood, which is a cleaner test than most of the names in this universe face.

Working Through the 14-Day Target

The 14-day target of $177.44 is 12.6% above the current $157.64. Against a 96.7 implied-volatility reading, that is a wide expected range, and it arrives after a 15.36% monthly run. The arithmetic is unfriendly: you are paying an elevated premium for a move that is generous in absolute terms but no longer cheap relative to a price that has already moved.

Short interest at 2.9% gives no squeeze assist, and the two sell ratings among 49 analysts are too small a block to represent real dissent. Earnings sentiment reads Positive. So the setup is fundamentally clean and tactically expensive, which is the honest summary of a score-93 name that has already had its month.

Key Takeaways

  • ServiceNow gained 15.36% over a month while SOXX fell 16.1% and the S&P 500 fell 1.43%.
  • It rose through the October 22 sector selloff, closing at $157.00 against $149.54 on October 20.
  • Score 93 with 5 days to a listed October 28 print, price $157.64, market cap $162.98B, and 2.9% short interest.
  • Implied volatility reads 96.7, roughly triple Amazon's 36.8, so the premium is expensive relative to the reward.
  • The 3-day target of $161.60 implies only about 2.5%, which is the number to size a short-dated structure against.

It is worth being explicit that a 15.36% monthly run and a 96.7 implied-volatility reading can coexist without either cancelling the other. Momentum and volatility are not opposites; a trending stock inside a high-volatility regime can keep trending and still hand you a 20% adverse day. The run tells you the direction has been one way. The volatility reading tells you the position has to be sized for the days it is not.

What to Do Next

Chasing a 15% run into a print is a different trade from buying a setup. If you want ServiceNow exposure, decide whether you are underwriting the company or the momentum, because after this month the two are no longer the same trade. A defined-risk structure sized to the 2.5% three-day expectation keeps the position survivable if the print is good but the multiple is not.

Watch whether the relative strength holds through the Federal Reserve meeting on October 27 to 28. ServiceNow has been insulated from the AI financing repricing, but it is not insulated from a discount-rate move, and this week it has to prove the insulation is about the business rather than about positioning.
