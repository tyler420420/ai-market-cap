Momentum Died on October 22. Here Is the Ledger

By AI Market Cap 2026-10-08 #pre-earnings

Every few months a trading thesis gets tested in public, and the useful thing about that is that the losers can read their own receipts. The pre-earnings momentum trade — buy the AI names showing relative strength into a print, sell the reaction — ran well through most of this cycle. On October 22, 2026 it stopped working, and it stopped for a reason that had nothing to do with any company’s results.

That is worth more than another bull case, because the failure is legible. The dates are known, the damage is measured, and the mechanism is identifiable. Here is what happened, in the order it can actually be verified.

The one-session break

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 6.72% to 10,731.98 on October 22, its worst session since June 23 and its lowest close since July 29 — 773.58 points, and roughly $200 billion out of the index’s 30 members. SOXX dropped 6.79% to 475.69, SMH 5.99% to 523.35. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.6%, its steepest daily drop since early June.

The trigger was Lam Research’s October 21 earnings call. The cause was not AI demand. Speaking at GTC Berlin the same morning, NVIDIA’s CEO described neocloud demand as “off the charts.” What repriced was the supply chain and the financing underneath the buildout, and a jump in Brent crude above $110 a barrel on inflation and borrowing-cost fears pushed the whole complex a little further.

Momentum positions carry one structural weakness: they treat the tape as an input rather than an output. On October 22 that assumption failed for an entire sector simultaneously, and no position sizing based on the individual company could have absorbed it.

The month-long ledger

Measured from Yahoo chart data, the roughly one-month moves through October 22 are the cleanest statement of what unwound. Ordered worst first, with where each name closed on the 22nd:

  • NBIS — Nebius Group: -29.76%. Closed $159.17, down 7.23% on the day. It reported October 22 before the open, against expectations of a loss of 67.28 rubles per share on revenue of 75.96 billion rubles.
  • LRCX — Lam Research: -25.10%. The name that started it, down 11.76% on October 22.
  • VRT — Vertiv: -21.38%. Closed $195.59, down 7.81% on the day, with its print six days away on October 28.
  • MU — Micron: -20.09%. Closed $856.49, down 6.26%. It has not reported and is no longer in the scanner universe; the Roundhill memory ETF fell about 7% that session, dragged by Micron, Sandisk, Seagate and Western Digital.
  • LITE — Lumentum: -19.69%. Closed $752.54, down 11.54% on the day, reporting November 5.
  • SMCI — Super Micro: -18.63%. Closed $33.72, down 5.20% on the day, with full results and the call scheduled for November 3 at 5:00 p.m. ET.
  • DELL: -17.62%. Closed $452.95, down 6.78%, and has not reported since September 1.
  • AMD: -17.36%. Closed $507.93, down 8.10%, with its print five weeks away.
  • CRDO — Credo Technology: -16.04%. Closed $162.77, down 10.70%, at a one-month low — after rising 6.28% the previous session.
  • AVGO — Broadcom: -8.94%. Closed $323.24, down 4.31%, the mildest outcome among the large AI names.

The control group: what did not break

Read the same table sideways and the dispersion is the real finding. IBM rose 0.26% on October 22 and is up 8.31% over about a month — on the day the entire AI complex fell. It had reported on October 21 with revenue of $17.1 billion, EPS of $2.95 against $2.86 expected, and free cash flow of $3.0 billion, up $0.7 billion year over year.

Tesla fell only 3.08% on October 22 and is down 1.46% over a month, the most resilient of the AI-adjacent complex. It had beaten on October 21 with third-quarter revenue of $28.05 billion against $28.24 billion a year earlier, and cut capital spending to $20 billion from $25 billion — the single most-quoted input in the AI capex dispute the next day. Being the loudest spender in the sector turned out to be defensive. Meta, also reporting October 21 with estimates beaten and full-year capex guidance largely unchanged, closed down 6.98% and sits about 12.8% lower over a month, because near-zero free cash flow had become the symbol of AI capex strain.

Same sector, same week, opposite outcomes. Momentum cannot tell you which side of that line a name lands on. It only tells you what recently happened, and what recently happened stopped being predictive on October 22.

The Super Micro case: momentum into a print, tested

SMCI is the cleanest natural experiment available, because the bullish catalyst and the bearish outcome are both on the record.

On October 20 it issued a preliminary fiscal Q1 FY2027 update: revenue expected at $18.5 billion to $19.5 billion against prior guidance of $14.5 billion to $15.5 billion, and gross margin of 12.0% to 12.5% against prior guidance of 10.4% to 10.8%. The stock jumped 11% to 12% after hours. That is the momentum trade working exactly as advertised.

Then look at what it cost. Two sessions later the stock was $33.72 and about 18.63% lower over a month. Super Micro had not yet reported its quarter, and the damage had nothing to do with the preliminary numbers, which were better than anything management had guided to. The prior quarter, reported August 11, had revenue of $11.12 billion — up 93.2% year over year, but short of the $11.56 billion consensus — alongside non-GAAP EPS of $1.70 against about $0.96 expected and more than $60 billion of orders. The balance sheet is where the momentum bid cracked: FY2026 operations used $6.8 billion of cash, the cash conversion cycle extended to 149 days, inventory rose to $12.9 billion, and Q4 free cash flow was $722 million against fiscal 2027 revenue guidance of $65 billion to $72 billion.

A trade that was right on the headline and wrong on the month is a signal about the buyer, not the company.

What the failure actually teaches

Momentum into a print only works when the sector tape cooperates. The condition is structural, not technical. A name like AMD can fall 8.10% in a session with its report five weeks away, and VRT can fall 7.81% with its report six days away, because neither move contained any information about either company. On that week, every single-day move in AI names was contaminated by one sector event and could not be read as an earnings reaction at all.

That also means the month-long damage cannot be attributed to fundamentals without more work. Lumentum is down 19.69% over a month and has not reported. Vertiv is down 21.38% and has not reported. Micron is down 20.09%, is not in the scanner universe, and has a print with no confirmed date available. Those three had no print to react to. What they had was a sector.

How to run the next four prints

Keep the trade, but drop the assumption. Before taking a pre-earnings momentum position, check what the sector did in the last five sessions. If the tape is being set by something exogenous — an equipment supply warning, an oil spike, a rate repricing — the momentum signal is not independent information and should not be counted twice.

Separate the two risks explicitly: the print risk you are taking a view on, and the sector risk you are not. Size the position against the second one, because on October 22 it was the second one that produced the entire loss.

Read the balance sheet before the beat. The Super Micro episode is the counterexample to the idea that a pre-announcement settles the question. Revenue guidance raised by several billion dollars did not protect a stock that carried a 149-day cash conversion cycle and $12.9 billion of inventory into a quarter it had not yet reported.

And keep dates straight, because the calendar is the one part you can verify. November 2 brings PLTR. November 3 brings AMD, ANET and ETN, plus Super Micro’s full results at 5:00 p.m. ET. November 5 brings Lumentum. November 9 brings Constellation Energy. November 12 brings Applied Materials. Dell’s next report is expected around November 24 but is not confirmed by the company, so treat it as an estimate rather than a date. Those are the checkpoints at which momentum either earns its place again or does not.
