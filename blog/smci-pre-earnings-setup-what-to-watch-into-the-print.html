Super Micro Pre-Earnings: Half the Print Is Already Out

By AI Market Cap 2026-10-20 #pre-earnings

Super Micro Computer has not reported its quarter. Full results and the call are scheduled for November 3, 2026, at 5:00 p.m. ET. Everyone agrees on that part. The unusual part is that a preliminary update already landed on October 20, which means the headline economics of the quarter are public and the balance sheet is not.

That splits this pre-earnings setup into two very different halves, and the half most of the coverage skipped is the half that will decide the stock.

Half the Print Is Already Public

The October 20 preliminary fiscal Q1 FY2027 update was a large raise. Revenue is expected at $18.5B–$19.5B against prior guidance of $14.5B–$15.5B. Gross margin is expected at 12.0%–12.5% against prior guidance of 10.4%–10.8%. The stock jumped 11–12% after hours.

Both changes point the same direction, and neither is marginal: the new ranges sit outside the old ones on both lines. Whatever ambiguity existed about the top line and the margin structure going into this quarter has been substantially resolved before November 3. The range the market trades against is no longer a guess.

Which leaves a strange asymmetry for anyone holding a position. The upside case is largely priced into the framing already published; the unresolved questions are the ones that decide whether the quarter was clean.

What that preliminary update did not include matters just as much. It carried no reported income statement, no balance sheet, no cash flow statement, and no revised fiscal 2027 framing beyond what was already on the record. A range of expectations is not a result. Until November 3, the arithmetic that connects $18.5B–$19.5B of revenue to actual cash is entirely unstated, and that is the arithmetic a fast-scaling hardware business lives or dies on.

The Quarter That Produced the Raise

The raise did not appear out of nowhere. The prior quarter, Q4 FY2026, reported on August 11, carried revenue of $11.12B, up 93.2% year over year — and still missed the $11.56B consensus. Non-GAAP EPS came in at $1.70 against roughly $0.96 expected. Order intake was more than $60B, and Q4 free cash flow was $722M.

Notice the shape of that combination. A quarter that nearly doubled revenue missed the top-line consensus anyway, and the company still had to guide the next quarter up sharply to produce the October reaction. Revenue in this business does not arrive cleanly in the quarter it is sold — which is exactly why a preliminary range is not the same thing as a reported quarter.

There is a second detail in that release that matters as much as the miss. Q4 free cash flow was $722M, a positive number on a quarter with $11.12B of revenue, while full-year operations consumed $6.8B of cash. Both can be true at once when a company is scaling inventory and receivables ahead of demand, and both were true in the same fiscal year.

The Fiscal 2027 Guide and the Cash Behind It

Fiscal 2027 revenue guidance is $65B–$72B. Against the Q4 FY2026 base, that is a step change in absolute scale, and it is the number that should govern how the November 3 call gets read.

The detail underneath is what deserves the attention:

  • Fiscal 2026 operations used $6.8B of cash.
  • The cash conversion cycle extended to 149 days.
  • Inventory rose to $12.9B.

Guidance was raised and cash was consumed to build the position that serves it. A 149-day conversion cycle and $12.9B of inventory are not automatically bad in a business scaling at this rate — inventory is a growth asset when demand turns out to be real. They are a precise set of questions, and unlike the revenue range, they have not been pre-answered.

Then the Tape Broke

By October 22, Super Micro closed at $33.72, down 5.20%, and sat about 18.63% lower over roughly a month. It had not reported, and it had no company-specific news to absorb.

The cause was Lam Research’s October 21 call, where CEO Tim Archer said clean room space availability and supply chain constraints for certain components will continue to limit chip makers’ and equipment companies’ ability to fully meet end demand as the industry moves into calendar 2027. CFO Doug Bettinger said the revenue guide “would have been a bit higher if not for some near-term supply chain constraints that we are actively managing,” with constrained revenue estimated at roughly $300M–$400M for the quarter and delayed shipments moving to the March quarter.

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 6.72% to 10,731.98 that day, a loss of 773.58 points, its worst session since June 23, 2026 and its lowest close since July 29, with roughly $200 billion removed from its thirty members. The iShares Semiconductor ETF dropped 6.79% and the VanEck Semiconductor ETF fell 5.99%. The Nasdaq Composite lost 2.6%, its steepest daily drop since early June, and the QQQ fell 2.86% — leaving it down 3.23% across the five sessions through October 22. The S&P 500 fell 1.21%. Meanwhile NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang, speaking at GTC Berlin the same morning, described neocloud demand as “off the charts.” Super Micro sells into that demand. It was marked down by someone else’s supply constraint, in a session that also pushed Brent crude above $110 a barrel, its highest since May, on inflation and borrowing-cost fears.

Two other names in the same scanner universe confirm the contamination. VRT fell 7.81% with its print six days away. AMD fell 8.10% with its print five weeks away. Neither had reported either. Super Micro’s 5.20% drop was, in that context, one of the milder outcomes in the group.

Keep those two facts side by side: the raise that was published, and the drawdown that followed without a print. They are not connected, and treating them as if they were is the most expensive misreading available here.

What November 3 Actually Turns On

A large raise is not the same as a clean quarter. The gap between those two things is where the risk sits, and almost all of it sits in the statements and the call rather than in the headline:

  • Does the published range hold as reported? The revenue and gross margin ranges are known; whether the printed quarter matches them is still open.
  • What does the cash conversion cycle do? 149 days is the most informative number in the last release, and direction matters more than level.
  • What is inventory doing against $12.9B? Growth that requires more inventory is a different story from growth that has started converting.
  • How is fiscal 2027 growth funded? Fiscal 2026 operations used $6.8B of cash, and the fiscal 2027 guide is $65B–$72B.
  • Does more than $60B of orders convert on schedule? Backlog is a claim on future quarters, and conversion timing is where the exposure lives.
  • Does the November 3 call get read against the supply narrative? The whole complex was repriced on supply, not demand, and the call is where that gets addressed directly.

Next Steps

Read the October 20 update as a partial print rather than as the event. The market already has a number to trade, so the surprise available on November 3 lives in the cash statement, the inventory line and the conversion commentary — not in the revenue range. Check the reported quarter against the $18.5B–$19.5B guide before reacting to any headline beat, and separate the guidance raise from the balance sheet, because those two can move in opposite directions in the same release. If you hold through the print, decide now whether you are underwriting the revenue guide or the cash profile. Carrying both views at once is how a raise gets erased by a working-capital line.
