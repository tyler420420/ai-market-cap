DDOG Pre-Earnings: Score 98 and a 23% Implied Range

By AI Market Cap 2026-10-23 #DDOG pre-earnings

Datadog scores 98, the highest reading in a 100-name scanner universe, with 12 days to a listed November 4 earnings date. It is also, right now, the most volatile setup on the board: an implied-volatility reading of 98.0 and a 14-day target of $315.67 against a price of $256.92, an implied range of 22.9%. The score and the volatility are telling you opposite things, and the trade is in the gap between them.

What the Score Is Made Of

The composite score runs to 100 and is built from analyst coverage, buy conviction, strong-buy count, upside to the short-dated target, and earnings sentiment history. Datadog scores 98 on the strength of 48 analysts rating it: 10 strong buy, 34 buy, 2 hold, 2 sell. Forty-four of 48 are non-negative, short interest is 3.3% of float, and the scanner marks earnings sentiment as Positive.

A 98 is close to the ceiling and it means the setup is well supported. It does not mean the move is large. Those are different questions, and conflating them is the most common way people lose money on a high-scoring name.

The Price Has Been Chaotic, Not Trending

Look at the last four sessions and the volatility reads clearly. $256.43 on October 20. $273.09 on October 21, a 6.49% gain. $257.75 on October 22, down 5.62% as the semiconductor complex collapsed. Then roughly $264 intraday on October 23, up 2.7% from the prior close.

In four sessions Datadog went up 6.49%, gave almost all of it back on a day when the SOXX fell 6.79%, and has now recovered most of it. Over roughly the past month the stock is down 1.28%. That is not a stock making new highs into a print. That is a stock being repriced violently by whatever the day's macro story happens to be, which is exactly what a 98.0 implied-volatility reading describes.

The Target Ladder Is the Real Story

The scanner's three targets for DDOG are unusually wide, and the shape between them matters:

  • 3-day target $268.67, about 4.6% above the current price of $256.92.
  • 7-day target $292.17, about 13.7% above.
  • 14-day target $315.67, about 22.9% above.

All three come from at-the-money straddle pricing at one, three, and five times the implied move. A ladder that runs from 4.6% to 22.9% is not a forecast of a 23% gain. It is a mathematical consequence of high implied volatility applied to a name 12 days from a print. The options market is pricing a wide distribution, and the ladder shows you how quickly the expected range expands with time.

That distinction is the practical edge. A trader anchoring on the $315.67 figure is anchoring on a straddle extrapolation, not on an analyst view or a company number.

Positioning for a Wide Distribution

The appropriate conclusion from a 98.0 implied-volatility reading is not that the stock is mispriced. It is that the position has to survive being wrong by a lot. A double-digit adverse move in a name like this is not a tail event; it is roughly what the straddle is paying for.

That means size matters more than direction here. The 3.3% short interest is unremarkable, so there is no squeeze to lean on. The two sell ratings out of 48 are noise. The genuine variable is how wide the reaction turns out to be, and the straddle structure is paying for exactly that uncertainty.

Score 98 in Context: Where the Rest of the Board Sits

Datadog's score of 98 is the highest in a 100-name universe, and it is worth seeing how little the score tells you about the size of the expected move. Twilio carries a score of 87 with an implied-volatility reading of 138.0, the widest on the board. Cloudflare is at 86 with 97.7. ServiceNow is at 93 with 96.7. Datadog is at 98 with 98.0. Microsoft is at 90 with 25.6. Amazon is at 92 with 36.8.

Those pairs make the point cleanly. A high score rewards analyst coverage, conviction and beat history. Implied volatility prices how wide the outcome distribution is. They are different measurements and they do not move together. Twilio is the most volatile setup on the board while scoring well below Datadog, and Microsoft scores 90 while pricing the narrowest distribution in the group.

So if you are choosing a name because its score is the highest, you are choosing the best-supported setup, not the biggest move. Datadog's 98 means forty-four of forty-eight analysts are non-negative and the company has been beating. It does not mean the stock is about to move 22.9%.

What Twelve Days Actually Buys You

With a listed November 4 date and 12 days out, Datadog sits in the middle of the scanner's stated 1-to-35-day entry window, and that middle position has a specific property: enough time for the market to keep repricing, without enough time for the premium to be fully determined.

The path over the last four sessions shows exactly that. A 6.49% gain on October 21, a 5.62% decline on October 22 when SOXX fell 6.79%, and a 2.7% recovery intraday on October 23. Over roughly the past month the stock is down 1.28%. A name moving that violently in a month that ends net down is being traded on daily macro narratives rather than on a directional view, and the 98.0 implied-volatility reading is the market's summary of that behaviour.

Twelve days out, the practical implication is that the setup can be repriced against you several more times before the print. That argues for a structure where the maximum loss is fixed at entry, and for sizing that assumes a double-digit adverse move is a normal outcome rather than a tail.

Key Takeaways

  • DDOG scores 98, the top of a 100-name universe, with 12 days to a listed November 4 print at $256.92 and a $92.25B market cap.
  • Implied volatility reads 98.0, making this one of the widest setups on the board.
  • The target ladder runs 4.6% at 3 days, 13.7% at 7 days, and 22.9% at 14 days, all straddle-derived rather than forecasts.
  • Four sessions of whipsaw: up 6.49% on October 21, down 5.62% on October 22, and up 2.7% intraday on October 23.
  • Short interest of 3.3% rules out a squeeze, so position sizing is the real control on risk.

One last input worth weighting: 3.3% short interest on a $92.25B market cap is a company large enough that borrow is a rounding error. There is no version of this trade where a crowded short unwinds and rescues a losing position. The scanner's 48-analyst book is the only real support underneath the price.

What to Do Next

Size the position so a double-digit adverse move does not end your analysis. At this implied-volatility level, direction is the easy call and sizing is the hard one. If you want to participate in the upside, use a structure where the maximum loss is defined before the print rather than after it.

And anchor to the 3-day target of $268.67 for short-dated structures. The 22.9% fourteen-day number is a straddle extrapolation that will look impressive in a summary table and misleading in a position. Twelve days is enough time for a lot of variance, and the volatility reading is the market telling you it expects a lot.
