NBIS: A 454% Revenue Quarter, a 29.76% Month

By AI Market Cap 2026-10-21 #pre-earnings

Nebius Group reported Q3 2026 results on October 22, before the market opened. By the close that same day the stock stood at $159.17, down 7.23%. Over roughly a month it is down 29.76% — the largest decline of any name in the October 22 closing table.

Very few names got that pairing. Nebius printed a result in the morning and then traded through a sector-wide liquidation in the afternoon, in the same session. That makes its October the most direct read available on how much of this complex's price action belongs to company fundamentals and how much belongs to one supplier's supply-chain remark.

This piece was originally written as a pre-earnings setup. It is now a result, and the result is more useful than the setup would have been: the operating question and the price question stopped agreeing with each other.

The only print-and-tape overlap on the calendar

On October 22 the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 6.72% to 10,731.98, its worst single session since June 23, 2026 and its lowest close since July 29. Roughly $200 billion came out of the 30 members' market value. The QQQ fell 2.86% and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.6%.

Nebius' 7.23% decline happened inside that session, so the day-level move is largely a sector move. The month-level number is where its own story lives, and it is worth comparing against the names that fell further on the day: Lam Research fell 11.76%, Lumentum 11.54%, Credo 10.70%, and CoreWeave 10% to sit about 39% below its early-October level. Nebius is down more over a month than any of them.

That comparison is the whole story. On the day, Nebius traded with the sector. Over a month, it has traded through a backdrop containing every one of those larger drawdowns and still given back more. The day tells you about October 22. The month tells you about the company.

Two quarters are in circulation, and only one of them is the print

Separating the figures matters here in a way it usually does not, because the more flattering number belongs to an earlier quarter:

  • The consensus going into the October 22 report: analysts expected a loss of 67.28 rubles per share on revenue of 75.96 billion rubles for the quarter.
  • The most recent quarter that actually printed: EPS of -$0.12 against a -$0.67 consensus — an 82.09% positive surprise — with revenue up 454% year over year.

Read the second line slowly. A loss of $0.12 against an expected loss of $0.67 is an unusually large beat measured in percentage terms, and it arrived alongside revenue growing 454% year over year. Whatever risk attaches to this business, it was not a demand disappointment in the last quarter that was actually measured.

One caution about how that 82.09% figure reads. A positive surprise measured against a loss per share says the loss was smaller than feared, not that the business earned money. Set against an expectation of -$0.67, printing -$0.12 is a large improvement expressed as a percentage and a modest one expressed in cents. The 454% revenue growth is the more consequential of the two disclosures, because it describes the volume of work the fleet is absorbing rather than the margin left once that work has been financed.

Growth at that magnitude also says nothing on its own about how the revenue is financed or how concentrated it is. Those are disclosures rather than inferences, and they belong in the next report instead of this article. What the measured quarter establishes is a floor: on the last occasion this business was observed directly, the demand side was not the problem.

The ruble-denominated expectation is the bar for the quarter reported on October 22. The reported outcome for that release is not in the verified figures behind this article, so no claim is made here about whether it beat or missed.

Demand was never the variable being marked down

The strongest evidence that October's move was not about AI demand comes from NVIDIA's CEO. Speaking at GTC Berlin on the morning of October 22 — the same morning Nebius reported — he described neocloud demand as "off the charts." The neocloud is precisely the business model Nebius operates in, and the most bullish statement available that week came from the supplier of its compute, not from its customers.

The repricing came from the other side of the ledger. Lam Research's CFO said the revenue guide "would have been a bit higher if not for some near-term supply chain constraints that we are actively managing," with roughly $300M–$400M of revenue constrained for the quarter and delayed shipments moving to the March quarter. Lam's CEO warned that moving into calendar year 2027, clean room space availability and supply chain constraints for certain components continue to limit chip makers' and equipment companies' ability to fully meet end demand, adding that the whole industry is going to have to work through very similar things.

When the binding constraint is physical capacity and demand is described as off the charts, the conclusion is not ambiguous. Demand was not what got marked down. The cost, timing and financing of fulfilling it were.

Funding structure is the constraint that binds

This is where the 29.76% month starts to make sense. A business growing revenue 454% year over year still has to fund the fleet producing that revenue, and October changed who is being financed into the buildout.

Three disclosures from the same tape:

  • CoreWeave fell 10% on October 22 and sat about 39% below its early-October level. CoreWeave is the closest listed comparison to what Nebius is building, and the market repriced its funding rather than its revenue.
  • Meta ran near-zero free cash flow while Alphabet carried historically negative free cash flow from its AI buildout. The largest buyers of AI capacity are funding it with balance sheets that generate little or no cash.
  • Tesla cut its full-year capital spending forecast to $20B from $25B after reporting on October 21. That single reduction became the most-quoted input in the capex dispute the next session.

Together those make a financing story rather than a usage story. When the suppliers of capital are being asked what they will fund and how they will fund it, a smaller neocloud whose fleet is financed to grow is where that question lands first and hardest. The 29.76% month is the market asking who funds a 454% grower, and the answer has not yet been yes.

What to watch next

One source lists the next expected release as November 10, 2026. Treat that as an estimate rather than a confirmed date; it has not been confirmed by the company in the figures behind this article.

Until then the useful checks are structural rather than technical:

  • Funding conditions for the listed neoclouds. CoreWeave stabilising would remove part of the reference point Nebius is being priced against.
  • Whether the supply-constraint narrative eases into 2027. That is the specific claim October discounted, and reversing it restores the growth story.
  • Nebius' move relative to the index. The 7.23% day is the sector; the 29.76% month is the company. Only one of them is company-specific.
  • Revenue growth against the 454% rate. A deceleration from a base this small is a different problem from a decline in demand, and the market will not price them the same way.

The summary is narrow on purpose. Nebius' operating result was strong on the last quarter that was measured, and the demand backdrop its customers operate in was described as "off the charts" the same morning it reported. What it lacks, and what a month of -29.76% is pricing, is a funding structure that does not depend on the market's appetite renewing itself every quarter.
