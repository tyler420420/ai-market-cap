STX Pre-Earnings: A 30% Month Into a 4-Day Window

By AI Market Cap 2026-10-23 #STX earnings date October 2026

Seagate Technology carries a scanner score of 85, a listed October 27 earnings date with 4 days to go, and a price of $630.34 against a market cap of $143.33B. It is also down about 30% over roughly the past month. A stock that has fallen that hard into a four-day window with a 96.3 implied-volatility reading is not a setup so much as a decision, and the decision has to be made on whether the fall was about the company or about everything around it.

The Month Was Violent in Both Directions

The path matters more than the endpoint. Seagate closed at $675.13 on October 20 and $696.89 on October 21. It then fell to $642.14 on October 22 as the semiconductor complex broke, and it sits near $641 intraday on October 23. Peak-to-trough inside three sessions that is roughly 8% of value removed, and it sits about 30% below where it was a month ago.

None of that is Seagate-specific news. The trigger across the storage and memory complex on October 22 was the Lam Research supply-chain commentary, the same remarks that took the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down 6.72% and roughly $200B out of thirty companies. Seagate sells drives; it does not fabricate wafers. The linkage is that both names are read by the market as AI-storage beneficiaries, so they were sold together.

What the Scanner Sees

Score 85 sits comfortably inside the top tier of a 100-name universe. Twenty-six analysts cover Seagate: 4 strong buy, 19 buy, 3 hold, 0 sell. Short interest is 3.1% of float, earnings sentiment reads Positive, and the sector is classified Technology.

The targets are the widest on the board for any name in this window. The 3-day target is $663.26, about 5.2% above the current price. The 7-day target is $729.10, about 15.7% above. The 14-day target is $794.94, roughly 26% above. The implied-volatility reading is 96.3.

A 26% fourteen-day implied range on a company with a $143.33B market cap is not a subtle signal. It is the options market saying it genuinely does not know what this print is worth, and it is the reason a 30% monthly drawdown is not automatically a buying opportunity even though it feels like one.

Why a 30% Drop Is Not Automatically a Setup

The pullback is being driven by sector contagion rather than by anything Seagate reported, and that cuts both ways. It is good news in the sense that no company-specific thesis has been invalidated. It is bad news in the sense that a name repriced 30% on someone else's call has not yet found where it settles, and four days is not much time for that process.

Contrast this with the names where the fall was genuinely informative. IBM rose on October 22 while the AI complex fell, having reported revenue of $17.1B and free cash flow of $3.0B. That is a print that repriced the stock. Seagate's month says almost nothing about its own quarter yet.

Four Days Is a Short Window to Re-Rate

The scanner's stated entry window runs from 1 to 35 days out. At 4 days, Seagate is in the compressed end of that window, where the premium is high and the time to be wrong is short. That is a different risk profile from the 25-day NVDA setup or the 12-day Datadog one.

The 5.2% three-day expectation is the number to size against, and at a 96.3 implied-volatility reading the premium required to capture it will be substantial. Anyone buying the 26% fourteen-day scenario is buying a straddle extrapolation, not an estimate of Seagate's quarter.

Storage Demand Was Not the Story Either

It is worth being precise about what was and was not repriced. The Roundhill Memory ETF fell about 7% on October 22. Micron closed at $856.49, down 6.26% on the day and about 20.1% over a month. Sandisk fell about 9%. Seagate fell about 8%. Western Digital fell to $354.19, and is down roughly 23.5% over a month.

None of those companies reported on October 22. Micron's last print was earlier in the year and no confirmed upcoming date is available. The entire complex moved on the same Lam Research sentence, which described clean-room space and component availability as an industry constraint into 2027.

That matters for how you read a 30% drawdown. A company-specific 30% fall usually means the market learned something about that company's business. A sector 30% fall means the market changed its mind about a narrative that several companies share. Seagate and Western Digital both fell hard in the same week without either reporting, and Seagate actually fell harder than Western Digital.

The Score and the Sentiment Split

Seagate scores 85, which puts it in the top fifteen of a 100-name universe. Twenty-six analysts cover it with 4 strong buy, 19 buy, 3 hold and 0 sell, so 23 of 26 are non-negative. Earnings sentiment reads Positive and short interest is 3.1%.

What that combination says is that the analyst community has not broken on Seagate while the tape has. A score of 85 with a Positive sentiment reading means the fundamental view is intact and the price disagrees for reasons that are not yet company-specific. In some setups that is a gift. In a four-day window with a 96.3 implied-volatility reading, it is an unresolved question with a very short fuse.

What a 26% Implied Range Actually Says

The fourteen-day target of $794.94 is 26% above the current $630.34. It is tempting to read that as a forecast of a recovery to new highs. It is not. It is a straddle extrapolation at five times the implied move, applied to a name with a 96.3 implied-volatility reading.

The honest way to use the ladder is as a volatility description. A 3-day expectation of 5.2%, a 7-day expectation of 15.7% and a 14-day expectation of 26% describe a market that prices enormous uncertainty around this print. They do not describe a company that is 26% likely to be worth $794.94. Any structure that needs the full 26% to pay for itself is positioned for the tail of that distribution, not for its centre.

Key Takeaways

  • Seagate scores 85 with 4 days to a listed October 27 print, at $630.34 and a $143.33B market cap.
  • It is down roughly 30% over about a month, but the move was driven by the Lam Research supply-chain shock rather than Seagate news.
  • Implied volatility reads 96.3, with a 3-day target of $663.26 and a 14-day target of $794.94.
  • Twenty-six analysts cover it with 4 strong buy, 19 buy, 3 hold and no sells, and short interest is 3.1%.
  • The October 22 fall was sector contagion, so it has not yet told you anything about Seagate's own quarter.

What to Do Next

Decide whether you are buying the storage complex or buying Seagate, because they are not the same thing this week. If the former, the memory and storage names were all repriced on the same sentence and any of them expresses it. If the latter, wait for a day that the sector is stable and let the stock find a level first.

A four-day window with a 96.3 implied-volatility reading rewards discipline over urgency. Size so that a double-digit adverse move does not end the position, and do not read a 26% implied range as a forecast.
