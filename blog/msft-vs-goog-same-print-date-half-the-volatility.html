MSFT vs GOOG: Same Print Date, Half the Volatility

By AI Market Cap 2026-10-23 #MSFT vs GOOG earnings

Microsoft and Alphabet report on the same day, are within $25B of each other on market cap, and carry the same scanner score of 90. The options market prices one of them at 25.6 implied volatility and the other at 55.6. That single number tells you more about how to trade this week than any amount of fundamental comparison, and almost nobody writes about it.

Two Names, Nearly Identical Profiles

Pull the two rows side by side from the live scanner and the similarity is genuinely striking. Microsoft: score 90, listed October 28 date, 5 days left, $520.23, market cap $3,862.95B. Alphabet: score 90, listed October 28 date, 5 days left, $317.86, market cap $3,887.41B. Same score, same day, same market cap to within half a percent.

The analyst books are close too. Microsoft has 57 covering it with 13 strong buy, 41 buy, 3 hold, 0 sell. Alphabet has 61 with 13 strong buy, 43 buy, 5 hold, 0 sell. Neither has a single sell rating. If you were picking on analyst conviction alone you would find no separation at all, and that is precisely why the volatility gap is informative.

The Volatility Gap Is the Whole Thesis

Microsoft's implied-volatility reading is 25.6. Alphabet's is 55.6. Alphabet is priced to move more than twice as far as Microsoft through the same week, on the same day, with comparable analyst support.

The scanner's targets reflect that directly. Microsoft: $526.42 at 3 days, about 1.2% above price; $538.81 at 7 days, about 3.6% above; $551.20 at 14 days, about 6.0% above. Alphabet: $322.50 at 3 days, about 1.5% above; $331.78 at 7 days, about 4.4% above; $341.06 at 14 days, about 7.3% above.

Notice that the 3-day expectations are nearly identical, 1.2% and 1.5%. The divergence is almost entirely in the longer-dated expectations, where Alphabet's implied range is consistently wider. The options market is not saying Alphabet has a worse quarter. It is saying Alphabet's outcome is less knowable, or that whoever owns it is more likely to be repriced.

What the Tape Did Differently

Over roughly the past month Microsoft is up 1.02%. Alphabet is down 5.97%. Both were hit on October 22: Microsoft fell from $532.95 to $521.08, about 2.23%, while Alphabet fell 3.08%. Microsoft has recovered to roughly $521 intraday on October 23; Alphabet is flat near $320.

That month-long divergence is the part that makes the pair interesting. Microsoft's relative calm is not because it avoided the AI financing debate. It is exposed to the same capex scrutiny. What it has instead is a shorter, more predictable path between its print and the next one, and the options market has priced that predictability at less than half Alphabet's rate.

Practical Consequences for a Pre-Earnings Trade

If you want exposure to the week with a defined risk ceiling, Microsoft is the cheaper structure in absolute premium terms. At 25.6 implied volatility you are paying roughly half what the Alphabet structure costs for the same expiry and a similar three-day expectation.

If you want the larger move, Alphabet is where the market says it is available, with a 7.3% fourteen-day expectation against Microsoft's 6.0%. That is a real difference, but it comes with a premium that scales with it. The trade is only interesting if the expected move exceeds what the premium consumes.

One caveat applies to both, and it is not a small one: the Federal Reserve meets October 27 to 28, the day before these prints. Neither company's volatility reading is purely company-specific right now. Some of the 30-point gap between Microsoft and Alphabet is the market assigning different amounts of that macro risk to each balance sheet.

Where the Two Books Actually Differ

The analyst distributions look identical until you count the hold ratings and the denominator. Alphabet has 61 covering analysts: 13 strong buy, 43 buy, 5 hold, zero sell. That means 56 of 61 are outright buy or strong buy, or about 92%. Microsoft has 57 covering: 13 strong buy, 41 buy, 3 hold, zero sell, so 54 of 57, or about 95%.

Both carry a Positive sentiment reading built from the last four quarters of results. Short interest reads 0% for Alphabet and 0.8% for Microsoft, both reflecting float size rather than bearish positioning. On every dimension that the scanner measures about analyst opinion, the two companies are close to interchangeable.

That is precisely why the 30-point volatility spread deserves attention. When everything measurable about the two setups looks the same and the market still prices one at more than double the other's expected movement, the spread is the market's genuine opinion about where the uncertainty sits. It is not measuring consensus. It is measuring dispersion of outcomes.

A Practical Ranking of the Two Setups

If forced to pick one for a defined-risk pre-earnings structure, the choice depends on which risk you are being paid for.

Microsoft at 25.6 is the cheaper way to hold a view on the AI capex week. Its 14-day target of $551.20 is about 6.0% above the current price, and the premium required to express it is roughly half what Alphabet demands. If your thesis is that enterprise AI spending continues at pace and you want to be right about the sector rather than about one company's margin structure, Microsoft is the efficient expression.

Alphabet at 55.6 is where the bigger move is being offered. Its 14-day target of $341.06 is about 7.3% above price, and the premium you pay scales accordingly. If your thesis is specifically about Alphabet's capital spending and free cash flow, the higher volatility is the market telling you that thesis is genuinely contested, which is information rather than a warning.

What neither expression captures cleanly is the Fed. A rate decision the day before both prints can move both names together, and in the same direction, regardless of what either company says. The company-specific spread is 30 points of implied volatility. The macro event sitting one day earlier is not priced into either number.

Key Takeaways

  • Both report October 28 with 5 days left, both score 90, and their market caps differ by under half a percent at $3,862.95B and $3,887.41B.
  • Implied volatility reads 25.6 for MSFT and 55.6 for GOOG, a spread of 30 points on the same expiry.
  • Three-day targets are nearly identical at about 1.2% and 1.5%; the gap sits almost entirely in the longer-dated expectations.
  • Microsoft is up 1.02% over a month while Alphabet is down 5.97%, despite similar analyst books with zero sell ratings on either.
  • The Federal Reserve meets October 27 to 28, one day before, so part of that spread is macro rather than company risk.

What to Do Next

Decide which risk you actually want before you decide which ticker. If you want the AI-capex week with the least premium attached, Microsoft at 25.6 is the cheaper way to hold the view. If you have a specific thesis about Alphabet's capital spending and believe the market has it too cheap, then the 55.6 reading is the cost of admission, not a signal to walk away.

Whichever you pick, size against the 3-day expectation rather than the 14-day one. The one-day gap between 1.2% and 7.3% is the entire reward, and the Fed meeting the day before is the risk that could compress both.
