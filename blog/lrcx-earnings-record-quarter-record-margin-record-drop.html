LRCX Earnings: Record Quarter, Record Margin, Record Drop

By AI Market Cap 2026-10-22 #AI stocks earnings October 2026

Lam Research just delivered the cleanest quarter in its history, and the market sold it off by roughly six and a half percent after hours. That combination — a record on every operating line, a beat on both headline numbers, and a double-digit-style drawdown — is the most useful thing that happened on the October 2026 AI earnings tape. It says more about where the AI capex cycle actually sits than any forecast did going in.

For context on why this matters beyond a single ticker: three of the five most-searched names this morning were all inside the same 24-hour printing window. IBM ($IBM) reported on October 21, Tesla ($TSLA) reported on October 21, and Lam ($LRCX) reported on October 21. All three landed at or above consensus. All three sold off. That is not three separate stories. It is one story about expectations.

LRCX Earnings: The Numbers Are Not In Dispute

Lam reported fiscal first-quarter 2027 results for the quarter ended September 27, 2026, after the close on October 21. Revenue was $8.22 billion, up 22% from the June quarter's $6.72 billion and up 54% year over year against $5.32 billion in the same quarter a year earlier. Non-GAAP diluted EPS came in at $2.20 against a consensus near $2.18, with GAAP diluted EPS of $2.19 and net income of $2.75 billion.

The margin lines are the real headline. Non-GAAP gross margin hit 52.4% and non-GAAP operating margin hit 40.2% — the first time Lam has cleared 40% at the operating line in its history. The June quarter gross margin was 52.0%. CEO Tim Archer and CFO Doug Bettinger have been building toward this for three years, and the September quarter is where it finally shows up in the reported numbers.

  • Revenue: $8.22B, up 22% QoQ and up 54% YoY
  • Non-GAAP EPS: $2.20 versus roughly $2.18 consensus
  • Non-GAAP gross margin: 52.4%, a record
  • Non-GAAP operating margin: 40.2%, first time above 40%
  • December-quarter guide: $8.6B revenue plus or minus $400M, EPS of $2.33 plus or minus $0.15

Why The Stock Fell Anyway

A beat does not create a rally when the bar has moved past the print. Lam guided December revenue to $8.6 billion at the midpoint, about 4.6% above the September quarter. For a company that just grew 54% year over year, that reads as a ceiling, not an acceleration. Shares fell roughly 6.45% in after-hours trading to around $243.99, with attention landing on supply chain constraints limiting how quickly Lam can ship into the surge rather than on demand.

This is the specific failure mode that pre-earnings momentum traders misread. The setup going into this print was textbook: Lam was beating, the WFE cycle was inflecting, and the AI capex narrative finally had a hard number attached. The stock still fell. When a beat-and-raise gets sold, the market is not rejecting the quarter. It is rejecting the gap between the quarter and the whisper.

The Number That Actually Matters: $200 Billion In 2027

Buried in the call was a figure worth more than every margin record combined. Lam now expects calendar 2026 wafer fabrication equipment spending in the mid-$150 billion range, and its preliminary 2027 forecast is above $200 billion. Management described 2027 percentage growth as led by DRAM, followed by leading-edge foundry logic and NAND.

That forecast moved through several upward revisions during 2026. At the Citi Global TMT Conference on September 9, CFO Doug Bettinger had 2026 WFE in the low-$150 billion range, up from $135 billion and then $140 billion earlier in the year. Clearing $200 billion for 2027 is a different order of magnitude, not another increment.

The leverage math is what makes it tradeable. Bettinger has framed roughly $100 billion of AI data center spending as converting into approximately $8 billion of WFE demand. Lam's share of addressable WFE is now probably north of 36%, up from the low-30s range. Hold that share against a $200 billion market and the served market alone implies a far larger revenue base than the December guide suggests. For scale, the Street was carrying full-year fiscal 2027 revenue near $34.95 billion, up about 50% year over year, with EPS around $9.49.

Watch the peers before you watch the multiple. Applied Materials ($AMAT), KLA ($KLAC), and ASML all report into the same December-quarter cycle. When three equipment names guide the same direction, you have a cycle. When one does, you have a company story.

What This Sets Up For The Next Pre-Earnings Window

Positioning into the December print is now a different problem than positioning into the September one. Lam has been clear that 2026 will be its third consecutive year of outgrowing the overall WFE market, with systems revenue up more than 40% at the midpoint of the December guide. Outperformance against a rising tide is worth more than outperformance against a flat one, because it tends to show up as share gain rather than cycle beta.

The risk to watch is not demand. It is delivery. The constraint flagged in after-hours trading was supply, which turns the December quarter into a test of whether shipments can catch up to orders rather than whether orders exist. For a pre-earnings trader, that is a fundamentally different setup than a demand-driven beat, and it deserves a different position size.

  • Long-term case: 2027 WFE above $200B with Lam's served share north of 36%
  • Near-term test: does December clear the $8.6B guide, and can it be shipped rather than booked
  • Sector confirmation: AMAT, KLAC and ASML guiding the same direction
  • Setup risk: after a 6% post-print drop, implied volatility into the December window is the cheapest it will get

Next Steps

Before the open, decide whether you are trading the 2027 WFE number or the December delivery question, because they are separate trades with separate failure modes. Then check what Applied Materials and KLA guide when they report: two confirmations turn this into a cycle trade, one confirmation leaves you owning a single-name supply chain story. If you want the cleanest version of the exposure, the December-quarter pre-earnings window on LRCX is the one to watch, with a stop defined below the post-print low rather than at an arbitrary percentage.
