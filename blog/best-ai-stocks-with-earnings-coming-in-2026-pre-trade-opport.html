The AI Earnings Calendar, Verified for Late 2026

By AI Market Cap 2026-10-08 #pre-earnings

Most earnings roundups are lists of companies someone expects to be interesting. This one is a list of dates that can be checked. As of October 23, 2026, the next four weeks of AI-related reporting are known, dated, and clustered into an unusually tight window — and the window sits directly on top of a Federal Reserve meeting.

That clustering is the reason to care. Between October 28 and November 17 there are at least a dozen AI names reporting, four of them inside the same forty-eight hours as a rate decision. Positioning a pre-earnings trade through that stretch is not a matter of picking the best name. It is a matter of knowing what is stacked on what.

What is actually on the calendar

Two published sources converge here. A published industry calendar from October 2026 lists Microsoft and Alphabet on October 28, Amazon and Intel on October 29, and the Federal Reserve meeting across October 27-28. The live scanner carries its own dates for twelve tracked names, which mostly agree. Lumentum is scheduled for November 5 on both. The live scanner adds several the industry calendar did not yet list.

October 27-28: the Fed sits inside the cluster

Start with the three prints on Wednesday October 28 and the two on Thursday October 29, because they land on top of the rate decision rather than around it.

  • MSFT — score 90. Reports October 28, five days out, at $521.08. The scanner’s implied-volatility reading is 27.1, the lowest in the group, with a P/E upside column of 1.3.
  • GOOG — score 91. Reports October 28, five days out, at $320.76. Implied-volatility reading 55.5, P/E upside 1.5. Alphabet carries historically negative free cash flow from its AI buildout, which is one of the specific things the market repriced on October 22.
  • VRT — score 83. Reports October 28, five days out, at $195.59. Implied-volatility reading 105.7, the highest of any name here, with P/E upside 3.3. VRT has not reported; it fell 7.81% on October 22 on the sector move alone.
  • KLAC — score 74. Also October 28, five days out, at $147.83. Implied-volatility reading 83.0, P/E upside 3.6. It fell at least 10% on October 22.
  • AMZN — score 93. Reports October 29, six days out, at $250.38. Implied-volatility reading 37.0, P/E upside 1.7. Amazon fell about 4% on October 22.

That is three hyperscaler-adjacent prints and two AI infrastructure prints inside forty-eight hours of a rate decision. The scoring already reflects the clustering risk in one respect: MSFT and AMZN carry the lowest implied-volatility readings in the table, while VRT and KLAC — the two with the shortest runway to their own print — carry the highest. The names priced for the most movement are the ones with the least time to absorb a macro surprise before they report.

November 2-17: the scheduled half

After the Fed week the calendar opens out. These dates are less congested, which makes them easier to hold through without an exogenous shock landing on top.

  • November 2 — PLTR. Score 74, ten days out, $196.19. Implied-volatility reading 57.0, P/E upside 2.7.
  • November 3 — AMD. Score 85, eleven days out, $507.93. Implied-volatility reading 77.5, P/E upside 3.2. AMD fell 8.10% on October 22 with its print still five weeks away, which is the clearest example of tape contaminating a single name.
  • November 3 — ANET. Score 93, eleven days out, $171.07. Implied-volatility reading 76.0, P/E upside 2.9.
  • November 3 — ETN. Score 78, eleven days out, $368.99. Implied-volatility reading 65.1, P/E upside 3.5.
  • November 3 — SMCI. Full results and the call at 5:00 p.m. ET. Not in the live scanner universe, so no score or price reading, but the date is fixed. It closed October 22 at $33.72.
  • November 5 — LITE. Not in the scanner universe. Confirmed on the published industry calendar, alongside Applied Optoelectronics. Closed October 22 at $752.54, down 11.54% on the day.
  • November 9 — CEG. Score 78, seventeen days out, $255.21. Implied-volatility reading 51.0, P/E upside 2.7.
  • November 12 — AMAT. Score 86, twenty days out, $375.58. Implied-volatility reading 61.4, P/E upside 4.6. It fell 8.2% on October 22.
  • November 17 — NVDA. Score 93, twenty-five days out, $212.21. Implied-volatility reading 44.8, P/E upside 1.9.

November 3 is the densest single day on the calendar: three scanner names plus Super Micro, all within one session. November 17 gives the window its clean ending, with the highest-scoring name in the universe reporting last.

Reading the implied-volatility column honestly

Every figure above labelled implied-volatility is the scanner’s implied-volatility reading, and it should be treated as exactly that. That column was mislabeled for a period and is only now re-verified. It is a number the scanner emits, not a claim about how any of these stocks is actually pricing a move, and nothing in a pre-earnings plan should rest on it without checking it independently.

The scanner’s score and P/E upside columns carry the same caution. A score of 93 on NVDA and a score of 74 on KLAC describe how the scanner ranks twelve names against each other on whatever inputs it uses. They are not forecasts. Both went into a week in which the whole complex lost six percent in a session.

The names with no confirmed date

Some of the most-watched AI names have nothing scheduled, and pretending otherwise is how calendars go wrong.

  • AVGO — Broadcom. Last reported September 2. Not on the published industry calendar’s list of set dates. Hock Tan has said supply is secured to roughly double AI revenue to about $115 billion in fiscal 2027.
  • CRDO — Credo Technology. Last reported September 1. Estimates point to roughly early December, which is an estimate, not a date.
  • MU — Micron. No confirmed earnings date is available. It is no longer in the scanner universe.
  • DELL. Next report expected around November 24, but the company has not confirmed it. Treat it as an estimate.
  • NBIS. One source lists November 10 as the next expected release. Also an estimate.

One honest discrepancy worth flagging: NVIDIA carries a November 17 date on the live scanner, while the published industry calendar from the same month still listed NVIDIA among the names that had not set dates. The scanner date is the more current of the two, but it is still a working figure rather than a company announcement.

How to run the next two weeks

Put the October 27-28 Fed meeting on the calendar as a marker first, then layer the prints on top of it. VRT and KLAC report the same day as Microsoft and Alphabet; ETN, ANET and AMD share November 3 with Super Micro. That is your correlation map, and it is more useful than any ranking of the twelve names against each other.

Decide now which risk you are taking in each name. A hyperscaler print and an equipment print inside the same Fed window are not the same trade, and owning both is not diversification.

Treat October 22 as the base case for how a position can move without any company-specific news. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 6.72% to 10,731.98, its worst session since June 23 and its lowest close since July 29, taking roughly $200 billion out of thirty index members. Any of these names can do that again on a supply sentence, an oil headline, or nothing at all.

And when the dates arrive, check them against reality before acting. The calendar above is accurate as of October 23, 2026 and was built to be correct rather than to look complete. Names move, dates get pulled forward, and a tracker that says “coming soon” tells you nothing.
