AI Stocks With Earnings in November 2026: Pre-Earnings Watchlist

The Q3 mega-cap print parade is wrapping up, and the calendar hands the baton to AI stocks with earnings in November 2026. If you build the watchlist now, you enter with a tape that's already leaning your way.

This piece breaks down which AI names report in November 2026, what score thresholds matter on our scanner, and how to set up the trade so IV crush doesn't eat your gains. We're not selling "long-term investing." We're flagging setups a smart trader friend would text you at 7 AM on a Tuesday.

Why AI Stocks With Earnings in November 2026 Are a Pre-Earnings Goldmine

November sits at the intersection of two forces that compound for the AI trade. First, the bulk of the year's AI capex prints by then — hyperscaler spend patterns, datacenter buildouts, and inference pricing are all out of the bag. Second, NVDA's print itself moves every name in the basket. SMCI, AMD, AVGO, CRDO, ARM, and the inference-platform plays all inherit the same momentum window. When NVDA squeezes, the cohort follows within 24–72 hours.

Three structural reasons make November prints cleaner than Q3 prints:

  • IV rank peaks earlier. Options dealers price NVDA's print volatility six to nine days out. By the time you see IV in the 70s on the scanner, the front-run crowd is already in.
  • Flow data front-runs the move. Unusual options sweeps into NVDA 14 days before the print hit the tape 9 of the last 11 quarters. Dark-pool prints follow 48 hours later. The scanner flags both signals.
  • The whisper-vs-consensus gap is widest in November. Hyperscaler capex whispers diverge from sell-side consensus by 6–14% on the November print cycle. That's where alpha lives.

How to Set Up the Trade: Score, IV, Flow, Entry Window

Three filters, applied in order, give you a clean pre-earnings shortlist. Anything missing one filter gets cut.

1. Combined score threshold. Our scanner blends technical (3-day and 5-day price action), sentiment (news flow, social), and options signals into a single 0–100 score. For AI stocks with earnings in November 2026, the floor is 80 for mega-caps (NVDA, CRM, ORCL), 85 for mid-caps (SMCI, CRDO, ARM), and 88+ for sub-$10B names where setup quality varies wildly.

2. IV rank between 35 and 70. Below 35, the options market isn't pricing the print as an event — there's no premium to capture. Above 70, IV is so rich that you'll lose the trade to crush even if direction is right. The sweet spot for a pre-earnings long-bias play is IV rank 45–60.

3. Options flow signal. Unusual call sweep activity into the front expiration, OR a dark-pool print over 0.5% of average daily volume on the run-up day. If neither fires within 5 days before the print, sit out — the trade doesn't have the fuel.

Entry window: open the position 4–8 trading days before the print. Exit: take half off at +6% from entry, take the rest off at +12% OR by the close before the print, whichever comes first. Hard stop at -5% from entry (close of day, not intraday).

What to Skip in November 2026

Three traps kill more pre-earnings trades than any single setup does. Avoid them.

  • Chasing IV rank above 75. The market is telling you the move is already priced. You'll get steamrolled by crush even if your direction is right.
  • Sizing big on low-float names. Sub-$500M float names (especially SOUN, BBAI) move 15% on a single day, but you can't exit at fair value when you need to. Size is the bug, not direction.
  • Holding through the print. Unless you're a long-term investor with a multi-quarter thesis, do not hold into the print. Sell the day before. The pre-earnings run-up is the trade. The print itself is a coinflip with worse odds than the run-up.
